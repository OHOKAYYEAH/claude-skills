# Shape: argument

The source as a case being made, so the reader can decide whether to believe it. It suits persuasive talks, debates, pitches, and opinion pieces. For a source that informs rather than argues, say so in one line and suggest the themes shape.

- `## Thesis`: 2–4 sentences, what the speaker wants you to believe or do, with its anchor. This is the TL;DR.
- `## Why they believe it`: a table with the columns **Supporting claim | Their evidence (anchored) | Kind of evidence**. Kinds include measured data, demo, personal experience, self-reported numbers, anecdote, historical analogy, and appeal to authority. Label the kind honestly: this column is the point of the shape.
- `## What they concede`: the limits, failures, open questions, and risks the speaker admits, each anchored. Write "None" if they concede nothing, because that is itself worth knowing.
- `## What they want you to do`: a numbered list of the concrete asks, each anchored. Skip the section when there are none.
- `## How strong is the case`: this shape's Reader's note, labelled as outside the source. Say which support is strongest and which is weakest, flag facts that look wrong, and name what's missing.
- `## Bottom Line`: 1–2 sentences, how persuasive it is, and why.
